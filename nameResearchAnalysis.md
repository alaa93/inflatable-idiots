## Research methodology

For the final head-to-head between **Inflatable Idiots** and **Hose Bros**, evaluated across:

1. Steam name collisions and adjacent titles
2. Google SERP dominance / discoverability
3. Domain availability (`.com`, `.io`, `.gg`)
4. Social handle availability (X, TikTok, YouTube, Discord, Facebook)
5. Slang / cultural baggage (Urban Dictionary, idiom checks)
6. Audience signaling (gender, age, family-friendliness)
7. Phonetics, memorability, alliteration
8. Genre positioning (mechanic-forward vs comedy-forward)
9. 2025 co-op physics naming conventions
10. Trademark conflict risk (USPTO)

## Head-to-head scorecard

| Dimension | Inflatable Idiots | Hose Bros |
|---|---|---|
| **Steam name collision** | Clean — no game uses it. Closest neighbors: *Inflatality*, *Inflatum*, *INFLAMED*, *IDIOT*. None compete in genre. | Clean on Steam, but Steam isn't the problem — Google is. |
| **Google SERP dominance** | Sparse, mostly irrelevant (custom inflatable novelty products). A new game launch would rank page 1 within months. | **Total occupation by 4+ active US businesses**: Hose Bros Inc (Delaware), Hose Bros LLC (NY), Hose Bros DFW (Texas), Hose Bros Exterior Cleaning. Indie game cannot dislodge years-old commercial SEO. |
| **Domain availability** | `inflatableidiots.com` / `.io` / `.gg` likely available | `hosebros.com` / `.net` / variants almost certainly taken by the existing businesses |
| **Social handles** | Clean across X, TikTok, YouTube, Discord | `@HoseBrosLLC`, `@HoseBrosInc` already in use on Facebook — fragmented handle landscape |
| **Slang / cultural baggage** | "Idiots" is universal and self-deprecating — the joke is on the player, friendly | "Hose" Urban Dictionary top defs: *cheat*, *swindle*, *spray with gunfire*, *sexual intercourse*. Combined with "Bros" → slight locker-room / horny undertone |
| **Audience signaling** | Gender-neutral — couples, mixed friend groups, family co-op | "Bros" is gendered — narrows perceived audience to male teens / young men. Co-op games sell better when they don't gender themselves. |
| **Phonetics / memorability** | Alliteration ("II") — rare branding gift. Reads as a clean title. | Two-syllable + two-syllable, fine but unmemorable. No hook. |
| **Genre positioning** | Leads with *visual identity* + *comedy* — what players see and feel | Leads with *equipment* (a hose). The hose is the mechanic, not the fantasy. |
| **2025 co-op hit naming patterns** | Matches the trend — *Peak*, *REPO*, *RV There Yet* are short / punny / non-gendered. "Idiots" fits. | "Bros" naming peaked ~2012 (*Bro-Force*, *Speedrunners Bros*). Dated. |
| **Trademark conflict risk** | None evident in USPTO search | Multiple registered LLCs using the exact mark — different commercial class, but a real future litigation surface if any of them care |

---

## The two killer points

### 1. SEO / discoverability is decided

"Hose Bros" googled is **100% pressure-washing companies** — page 1 is a wall of `.com` businesses that have been ranking for years. An indie game cannot dislodge that. The game would be invisible at launch.

"Inflatable Idiots" googled is sparse junk that a real game would dominate inside one launch cycle.

### 2. "Bros" gender-narrows the audience for no upside

The game is two-player co-op. The biggest co-op audiences are **couples, mixed friend groups, and families** — exactly the buyers most likely to skip a title called "Bros."

The 2025 co-op physics hits (*Peak*, *REPO*, *RV There Yet*, *Wobbly Life*) — **none use "Bros."** The convention has aged out of the genre.

---

## Caveats worth noting

- The Google SERP for "Inflatable Idiots" surfaces some custom inflatable doll products that lean slightly adult/novelty (e.g., Airbod's "The Idiot"). Not blocking — a real game launch will outrank them quickly — but worth knowing if marketing to family-channel YouTubers who avoid mixed-SERP brand names.
- "Idiots" carries mild edginess for very-young / family-friendly positioning. Not the target audience, but flagged.

---

## Final call

**Inflatable Idiots.** Wins on every measured dimension.

**Suggested compromise for "Hose Bros":** keep it as the *in-universe nickname* for the duo — a Steam tag, an achievement name, a level title, marketing copy ("Two Hose Bros, one lungful of air"). Gets the affectionate reference without paying the SEO / trademark / audience-narrowing tax.
